Research

Making context explicit

Context gating in predictive language processing

My dissertation tests whether the working-memory gating mechanisms associated with prefrontal cortex support prediction during real-time language comprehension. I combine matched neural language models, naturalistic EEG, and biologically grounded working-memory models.

Gating utility—the predictive benefit of an LSTM relative to a matched RNN—rises where independent psycholinguistic accounts predict greater working-memory demand. In EEG recorded while participants listened to Natural Stories, it predicts a later response distinct from the surprisal–N400 effect.

Human-autonomy teaming in aviation

At NASA Ames, I analyze how remote pilots respond to autonomous detect-and-avoid alerts and examine how alert type, workload, and physiological measures shape response time. Previously at Wisk Aero, I led rapid prototype studies using eye-tracking, physiological sensors, and qualitative methods.

I am interested in research roles in conversational AI, model evaluation, human factors, and human–AI interaction.
